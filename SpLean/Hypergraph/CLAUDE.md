# SpLean.Hypergraph

A ZX diagram as a combinatorial object, and the proof that two terms with
isomorphic hypergraphs are equivalent. A third representation alongside
`Axiomatic/` and `Algebraic/`, and **it imports neither** — they lower *into*
it, exactly as both lower into `SpLean/Widget.lean` for rendering. This is the
semantic meeting point; that one is the rendering meeting point.

Only `Algebraic/` lowers into it (`Algebraic/ToHypergraph.lean`). There is no
plan to lower `Axiomatic/` into it too: `Axiomatic/` is kept as a reference for
the proof ergonomics to aim for, not as something to be proved sound (see the
root `CLAUDE.md`).

## What it is for

`zx_iso` (`Algebraic/IsoTactic.lean`) closes `x ≈zx y` for closed terms with no
isomorphism supplied. That settles the question a derivation is bad at — *do
these two terms describe the same diagram?* — and says nothing about rules that
*change* the diagram. Fusion, colour change, Hopf and the rest stay rules.

`SemanticsTesting/10Hypergraph.lean` is the worked example:
`Gate.CNOT ≈zx Gate.CNOT'` in one tactic call, resting on no `sorry`, against
the same fact proved in `09Rules.lean` by a twelve-line derivation that rests
on two.

## The encoding: wires are vertices, generators are hyperedges

The **incidence dual** of the picture the renderer draws:

- a **vertex** is a *wire*, including ones the term never names — the wire
  between the two spiders in `Z ≫ Z` is a vertex;
- a **hyperedge** (`boxes`) is a *generator*, carrying its label and the wires
  on its legs;
- the **boundary** says which wire each input and output port is.

That way round because it makes `compose` a *vertex merge*: `a ≫ b` is the
disjoint union with `a`'s output wire `i` identified with `b`'s input wire `i`.
Generators-as-vertices would have to merge edges and renumber. The payoff shows
up in `sem_toHyp`'s `compose` case, where the merged vertices are exactly what
`ZX.sem`'s `∑ g` ranges over, term for term.

`ZX.wire` is one vertex and no hyperedges, with `inputs = [v]` and
`outputs = [v]` — so **the boundary maps are not injective in general**, and
that is what makes the unit laws come out as isomorphisms for free.

**Nothing here is a `List`.** `Box.arity` is a field rather than the length of
a leg list, and `boxes`/`ids` are `Fin`-indexed. A `List` puts `List.length`
into a type index, and every lemma about a box's tensor then needs a dependent
rewrite along `List.length_ofFn`. The phase 0 spike changed this after hitting
it once; phase 1 applied the same lesson twice more.

## Identifications, not a quotient

`compose` does not renumber. It records the merged pair in `ids`, and
`Hyp.sem` carries a `[a v = a v']` factor per pair. **The delta is what stops a
merged-away wire being summed over freely and doubling the denotation** — that
is the trap, and `sem_toHyp_wire_compose` is the smallest test that catches it.

The cost is that a `Hyp` is not a normal form, so `Iso` works up to `Hyp.Rel`,
the equivalence generated by `ids`. This is not a nicety. In `toHyp Gate.CNOT`
the Z spider's input leg *is* the boundary input wire, while in
`toHyp Gate.CNOT'` the boundary input is a separate wire identified with that
leg — no bijection matches those two, and they are obviously the same diagram.
Stating everything up to `Rel` also lets an isomorphism relate hypergraphs with
different wire counts, which is needed the moment one side is composed with a
bare `wire`.

## The one condition that cannot be relaxed

`Iso` fixes the boundary **pointwise and in order**. An isomorphism free to
permute boundary ports proves `swap ≈zx wire ⊗ wire`, which is false.
`sem_swap_ne_sem_parallel` in `Iso.lean` is the standing test: `swapHyp` and
`parallelHyp` are written by hand (this module imports no `ZX`), their
denotations differ, so by `Iso.sem_eq` no isomorphism between them exists. Any
future weakening of `Iso` that admits one stops the file compiling.

## Files

- `Defs.lean` — `Colour`, `Label`, `Box`, `Hyp`, `Hyp.Sat`.
- `Semantics.lean` — `Hyp.sem`, and that a box's tensor does not care what
  order its legs come in (`zTensor_congr` and friends, stated across two
  arities via an `Equiv` because that is the shape `Iso` produces). **This is
  where spider symmetry — which the term calculus cannot express — comes
  from.**
- `Iso.lean` — `Hyp.Rel`, `Iso`, and `Iso.sem_eq`: isomorphic hypergraphs
  denote the same tensor. The proof is one `Fintype.sum_equiv`, over the
  assignments that respect the identifications, on which the two wire maps
  really are mutually inverse.
- `Decide.lean` — `Hyp.rep` and `Iso.ofRepEq`/`Iso.ofTables`, the checkable
  forms.
- `Search.lean` — finding an isomorphism. Ordinary computable Lean, not meta
  code, so it can be `#eval`ed.

## Soundness sits in two theorems

`Iso.sem_eq` and `Algebraic/ToHypergraph.lean`'s `sem_toHyp`. Everything else
is convenience:

- **`Hyp.rep` is proved sound, not complete.** It is one pass of quick-find
  over `ids`, and what is proved is `H.Rel u (H.rep u)`, hence
  `rep u = rep v → H.Rel u v`. The converse is true but deliberately unproved:
  a certificate is *accepted* when representatives agree, so only this
  direction could make the check unsound. Incompleteness would at worst cost a
  certificate that fails to check.
- **`rep` threads a table through a `foldl`, and must keep doing so.** Written
  as a recursion on a function it needs the previous level at three places (the
  test and both branches), which costs `3 ^ idCount` to evaluate — invisible on
  CNOT's three identifications, and a ten-minute hang on a diagram with twenty.
  That was a real regression, found by the first `zx_iso` on
  `SemanticsTesting/11Ex37.lean`.
- **The search is untrusted.** `Iso.ofTables` re-checks every condition, so a
  wrong answer costs a failed tactic, never a bad proof. It could be replaced
  wholesale without a soundness argument.

## Two gotchas worth knowing before editing

- **`Box.bits b x` has type `Bits b.arity`**, so `simp` will not rewrite `b`
  underneath it — the motive is not type correct. Move the whole
  `Label.tensor _ b.label (b.bits _)` unit instead (`tensor_congr_box`).
  Relatedly `(Fin.addCases ..).label` does not even elaborate when written by
  hand, which is why `appendBoxes`/`appendIds` are named functions with
  `_left`/`_right` simp lemmas rather than bare `Fin.addCases`.
- **`decide` cannot evaluate `ℚ` equality in the kernel** (`Rat`'s operations
  go through `Nat.gcd`). It does not bite on phase-free diagrams, so CNOT never
  showed it; the `Gate.T`/`Gate.S` examples did. Matched boxes have
  syntactically equal labels, so `zx_iso` discharges the label condition with
  `rfl`. If more conditions grow phases in them, state them over a
  `ℕ × ℤ × ℕ` key rather than over `Label`.

## What this route does not reach

- **Anything that changes the graph.** The snakes in `Rules/Yank.lean` are not
  structural: `(cup ⊗ wire) ≫ (wire ⊗ cap)` has two spider boxes and `wire`
  has none, so the hypergraphs are not isomorphic. That equality is a fact
  about what a phase-free two-legged Z spider denotes.
- **Laws quantified over arbitrary diagrams.** `decide` has nothing to compute
  on, and building the isomorphism by hand means block permutations of `Fin`
  with all the attendant bookkeeping — where the semantics just factors.
  `Rules/Structural.lean`'s laws were proved semantically for that reason, and
  the attempt at the other route (`Iso.ofStrict`) was deleted for want of a
  caller. **The rule of thumb: hypergraphs when the diagram is fixed, semantics
  when it is a variable.**
- **Open terms.** `zx_iso` evaluates both sides, so a diagram parameterised by
  a phase has no hypergraph to compute. It checks and says so.

The tactic raises `maxRecDepth` itself before elaborating the certificate: the
`decide`s evaluate both hypergraphs, and past a couple of spiders that goes
deeper than the default.

`SemanticsTesting/11Ex37.lean` is the larger worked example — a diagram
rearranged until its π phases sit on the output wires, alternating `zx_iso` for
the seven rebracketings with `zx_rw` for the nine rules. It is the best
illustration of what this module buys: the structural steps, which used to
dominate a derivation, are one tactic call each.

## Not done

- Nothing rewrites yet. `Iso` proves two terms equal; DPO rewriting — which
  this encoding is the standard presentation for — would use it to *transform*
  one.
- A normal form absorbing fusion (connected same-colour spiders merge) would
  move fusion into the isomorphism check too.
- `Hyp → Wire.Diagram` would let `#zx` draw a hypergraph.
